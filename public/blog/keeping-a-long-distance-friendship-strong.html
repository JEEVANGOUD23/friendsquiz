How to Keep a Friendship Strong When You Live Far Apart

By the FriendMatch team · Updated October 2026

Moving to another city for study or work is a normal part of growing up, but it can put a strain on the friendships we leave behind. The good news is that distance does not have to end a friendship. With a little effort from both sides, it can even make you appreciate each other more. Here are some practical ways to keep the connection alive.

Set a regular time to talk

Waiting for the perfect moment to call usually means the call never happens. Choose a recurring slot, such as every second Sunday evening, and treat it like any other appointment. Even a 20-minute chat keeps you up to date on each other's lives.

Use voice notes when schedules clash

Time zones and busy days make live calls hard. Voice notes let you share news and jokes at your own pace. They also carry tone and laughter in a way that text does not.

Share the small, everyday things

Big updates are easy to share. The small ones are what make a friendship feel close: a photo of your lunch, a funny sign you saw, a song stuck in your head. Sharing ordinary moments helps you feel like part of each other's daily life.

Do something together, even remotely

Watch the same series and message each other about the episodes. Play an online game. Cook the same recipe over a video call. Or try a friendship quiz and see how well you still know each other. Having an activity gives you something to talk about besides catching up.

Send small surprises

A postcard, a snack from your city or a handwritten note costs very little and means a lot. Unexpected gestures remind your friend that you are thinking of them.

Remember the important dates

Birthdays, exam results, job interviews and anniversaries matter. A short message on the right day shows you care. Put the key dates in your calendar so they do not slip past.

Be honest about busy periods

Everyone has weeks when they disappear. A quick "I'm swamped, but I'm thinking of you" prevents misunderstandings. Silence without explanation is what makes friends feel forgotten.

Plan visits and look forward to them

Having a date in the diary changes how a friendship feels. Even if the trip is months away, planning it together gives you something to look forward to. Meeting in person also refreshes the bond in a way that screens cannot.

Do not keep score

One of you may be better at keeping in touch, and that is fine. If you notice you are always the one reaching out, mention it kindly instead of building up resentment. Most of the time your friend simply needs a nudge.

Accept that the friendship may look different

You will probably talk less than when you lived nearby, and that is normal. What matters is that the contact is genuine. A friendship does not need constant messaging to be strong, just steady care over time.
