How to Test Your Best Friend: 7 Fun Ways to See How Well You Know Each Other

By the FriendMatch team · Updated October 2026

Most of us are sure we know our best friends inside out. We know their coffee order, their ringtone and the exact face they pull when a joke isn't funny. But ask a few specific questions, such as their dream holiday, the season they like most, or what they would do with a free weekend, and the answers are not always what we expect.

Testing how well you know each other is a gentle, funny way to learn new things about someone you already love. Here are seven ways to do it, none of which need any preparation.

1. Take a friendship quiz with real answers

The simplest method is a quiz where one person answers honestly and the other tries to guess. Each question has a few options, and your friend has to pick the one you chose. It works well because the questions are small and easy to answer, yet the results spark long conversations. 2. Play "two truths and a lie" with small details

Instead of dramatic stories, use everyday facts. Say three things about your own habits, such as your favourite snack or the last film you watched, and let your friend spot the false one. Small details are harder to fake than big stories, and a friend who really pays attention will notice.

3. Guess each other's orders

Next time you are at a restaurant or café, write down what you think your friend will order before the waiter arrives. Compare notes afterwards. It sounds silly, but a surprising number of people get it wrong, and it makes for a good laugh.

4. Take a walk down memory lane

Ask each other about shared memories: where you first met, the funniest trip you took together, or the first thing you ever argued about. Friends often remember the same event in very different ways, and comparing versions is half the fun.

5. Swap playlists

Each of you builds a short playlist of songs you think the other person loves. Then listen together and see how many hit the mark. Music taste says a lot about a person, and the misses are usually the most entertaining part.

6. Make predictions about the future

Write down a few predictions about your friend, such as where they will be living in five years or what job they will have, and seal them in an envelope. Open them on a set date. It is a lovely keepsake and a reason to check in later.

7. Ask the deeper questions

Once the light-hearted games are done, try questions about values, worries and dreams. What does your friend want to achieve this year? What is something they are nervous about? These conversations rarely happen by accident, and they are what turn a good friendship into a close one.

Keep it fun, not competitive

The point is not to win. If someone scores low, treat it as a chance to learn something, not as a failure. A low score simply means there is more to discover, and that is a nice thing to have in a friendship.

What to do with the results

Pay attention to the questions your friend got wrong. They often point to topics you have never talked about. Pick one and ask a follow-up question. You may find a shared interest or a story you had never heard.
