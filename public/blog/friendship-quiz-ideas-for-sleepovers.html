12 Friendship Quiz Ideas for Sleepovers and Hangouts

By the FriendMatch team · Updated October 2026

A good sleepover or hangout does not need a plan, but a few games can keep the evening from drifting into everyone scrolling on their phones. These twelve friendship quiz ideas need nothing more than a few people, some snacks and a bit of imagination.

Quick games (about 10 minutes each)

1. The favourites quiz

One person answers questions about their favourite colour, food, season and so on. Everyone else guesses. You can do this on paper, or set it up online with a tool like FriendMatch, where each person gets their own shareable quiz link.

2. This or that

Ask rapid-fire either/or questions: tea or coffee, beach or mountains, morning or night. Everyone answers at the same time by pointing left or right. It is quick, and the disagreements start good debates.

3. Finish the sentence

Start a sentence such as "The best part of weekends is..." and have each person complete it in turn. The answers are usually funnier than you expect.

4. Most likely to...

Read out a prompt like "most likely to get lost with a map" and everyone points at the friend they think fits best. Keep the prompts light and kind.

Team games (for bigger groups)

5. Who said it?

Each person writes down a funny thing they have said recently. Read them out and let the group guess who said what.

6. Baby photo guessing

Everyone brings a childhood photo. Mix them up and guess who is who. The expressions alone are worth it.

7. Pop culture duel

Split into two teams and trade questions about films, songs and shows you all know. Choose topics everyone enjoys, so nobody feels left out.

8. Mystery bag

Each person puts a small personal object, such as a keyring or a favourite snack, into a bag. Pull them out one at a time and guess whose it is.

Creative challenges

9. Blind taste test

Blindfold a friend and let them guess snacks or drinks. Pick safe, simple foods and check for allergies first.

10. Draw each other

Give everyone one minute to draw the person on their left. The worse your drawing skills, the better the game.

11. Memory timeline

Together, build a timeline of the group's shared history on paper, with the first meeting, the best trip and the funniest moment. People fill in the gaps for each other.

12. The compliment round

End the night with a kind game. Each person says one thing they appreciate about the friend on their left. It is a warm way to finish, and people remember it.

Tips for a good game night

  • Keep scores light. Prizes can be silly, like choosing the next film.
  • Mix the teams. Pair friends who do not usually sit together.
  • Avoid embarrassing secrets. The goal is laughter, not discomfort.
  • Know when to stop. End a game while people are still enjoying it.
